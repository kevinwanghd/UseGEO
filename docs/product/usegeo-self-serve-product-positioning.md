# UseGEO Self-Serve Product Positioning

## 1. Document Purpose

This document defines the target positioning for UseGEO as an overseas-only,
solo-operated, low-touch digital product. It is the strategic input for the next
revision of the main PRD. It does not replace the implementation PRDs for the
scanner, commerce, or report-delivery modules.

## 2. Strategic Decision

UseGEO will move from a mixed model of content publishing, lead generation,
consulting handoff, partner referral, and sponsor inventory to one primary
business:

> A self-serve GEO readiness audit that sells an implementation-ready report to
> small English-speaking B2B SaaS teams.

The core product will be purchased once, delivered automatically, and usable
without an account, sales call, consulting engagement, or recurring
subscription.

UseGEO may continue publishing educational content as a customer-acquisition
channel. Content, sponsors, community submissions, partner matching, and lead
resale are not part of the core product or its primary revenue model.

## 3. Positioning Statement

### 3.1 Value Proposition

**For** founders and marketing owners at 5-50 person B2B SaaS companies with an
English-language marketing website,

**who need** to know exactly what to change so their website is easier for AI
systems to understand, extract, and cite,

**UseGEO is a** self-serve GEO readiness audit

**that** turns evidence from three critical website pages into exact copy,
schema, content-module, and prioritization recommendations that a customer can
implement immediately.

### 3.2 Differentiation

**Unlike** free GEO graders that produce generic scores and monitoring platforms
that require an ongoing subscription,

**UseGEO provides** a one-time, implementation-ready action report with page-level
evidence and concrete fixes, without a dashboard, agency engagement, or sales
call.

### 3.3 Customer-Facing One-Liner

> Turn your B2B website into an AI-ready source with exact fixes, not another
> dashboard.

### 3.4 Supporting Message

> Audit three critical pages and get exact copy, schema, evidence, and a
> prioritized implementation plan. One payment. No subscription. No sales call.

## 4. Product Category and Naming

The preferred category is:

- GEO Readiness Audit
- AI Citation Readiness Audit
- Implementation-Ready GEO Audit

UseGEO should not describe the current product as:

- an AI ranking tracker;
- an AI visibility monitoring platform;
- a ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity ranking checker;
- a full-service GEO agency;
- a generic B2B growth content site.

The current scanner evaluates whether page content contains signals that make it
clearer and easier to extract. It does not continuously measure real prompt-level
brand visibility or guarantee citations and rankings.

## 5. Ideal Customer Profile

### 5.1 Primary Customer

- Geography: United States, Canada, United Kingdom, Western Europe, and
  Australia.
- Company type: B2B SaaS.
- Company size: approximately 5-50 employees.
- Website: English-language site with an established homepage and product or
  service pages.
- Buyer: founder, head of marketing, growth lead, or content/SEO owner.
- Situation: aware of AI search and GEO, but not ready to buy an expensive
  monitoring platform or agency engagement.
- Capability: able to implement changes directly in a CMS or hand a structured
  report to a developer or content editor.
- Buying job: obtain a clear, one-time implementation plan for improving the
  website's GEO readiness.

### 5.2 Customers Not Served Initially

- local restaurants, clinics, and other local-search-first businesses;
- personal bloggers and hobby sites;
- large enterprises requiring procurement, security reviews, or team features;
- ecommerce catalogs requiring product-level monitoring;
- agencies requiring white-label reports, bulk scans, workspaces, or APIs;
- customers seeking guaranteed AI rankings or ongoing brand mention tracking;
- customers requiring consulting, meetings, or done-for-you implementation.

## 6. Customer Problem

The primary problem is not simply that customers lack an AI visibility score.
Free tools already provide basic scores.

The underserved customer needs are:

1. The customer does not know whether AI systems can clearly identify the
   company, category, product, proof, and use cases from its website.
2. Free graders identify problems but rarely show exactly what the customer
   should write, add, remove, or restructure.
3. Subscription visibility platforms are too expensive and operationally heavy
   for a team that first needs to improve its website foundations.
4. Manual audits and agencies require calls, proposals, long delivery times, and
   significantly higher budgets.
5. The customer needs a report that can be implemented directly or handed to a
   developer without additional explanation.

The customer is buying reduced uncertainty and implementation speed, not a
decorative score.

## 7. Product Promise and Boundaries

### 7.1 Product Promise

UseGEO will:

- inspect real content from the submitted pages;
- show evidence supporting each important finding;
- distinguish findings from recommendations;
- generate concrete copy, schema, and content-structure changes;
- prioritize changes by expected impact and implementation effort;
- provide a practical 30-day implementation sequence;
- deliver the purchased report automatically by protected link and email.

### 7.2 Explicit Non-Promises

UseGEO will not:

- guarantee mentions, citations, traffic, leads, or rankings;
- claim to measure continuous visibility in ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, or other
  answer engines unless that capability is actually implemented;
- represent readiness scores as real market share or share of voice;
- provide legal, financial, or business-performance guarantees;
- include human consulting, implementation, or custom analysis in the standard
  product.

## 8. Offer and Packaging

### 8.1 Free Scan

The free scan evaluates one page, normally the homepage, and shows:

- the total readiness score;
- five dimension scores;
- three important findings;
- at least one real page-level evidence item for each finding;
- a concise explanation of the scoring method and its limitations;
- a preview of the paid implementation report.

The free result must be useful enough to establish trust. It should diagnose the
problem without giving away the complete implementation package.

### 8.2 Paid Product

Working offer:

**UseGEO Action Report - USD 79 one-time**

The paid report covers three critical pages:

1. homepage;
2. primary product or service page;
3. pricing, about, comparison, or another customer-selected commercial page.

The report includes:

- real evidence collected from all scanned pages;
- prioritized findings across the five readiness dimensions;
- recommended title, meta description, and H1 changes;
- proposed homepage or product-page positioning copy where relevant;
- copy-ready structured-data examples;
- proof, comparison, use-case, FAQ, and content-module recommendations;
- internal-linking and information-architecture recommendations where supported
  by the scan;
- a 30-day action plan ordered by impact and effort;
- one automated rescan within 30 days;
- a protected web report with print-to-PDF support;
- email delivery and report-link recovery.

The launch should use one paid tier. Price experiments may test USD 59-99 after
real purchase data exists. The product should not launch with a complex pricing
table.

## 9. Business Model

The primary commercial path is:

```text
Search or educational content
-> free homepage scan
-> evidence-backed result
-> one-time checkout
-> three-page action report
-> email delivery
-> one automated rescan
```

Revenue comes from one-time digital report purchases. The payment provider should
support automated global checkout, tax handling, invoices, refunds, and webhook
delivery for a China-based operator selling overseas.

The initial business model excludes:

- consulting revenue;
- implementation services;
- lead resale;
- sponsor inventory as a primary revenue source;
- partner referral as a primary revenue source;
- advertising;
- recurring subscriptions.

## 10. Product Scope

### 10.1 In Scope

- English-language B2B SaaS websites;
- free single-page readiness scan;
- paid three-page implementation audit;
- real page fetching, parsing, rule scoring, and evidence capture;
- LLM-generated recommendations constrained by captured evidence;
- exact copy and structured-data suggestions;
- impact and effort prioritization;
- 30-day implementation plan;
- one-time automated checkout;
- payment-webhook verification and idempotent fulfillment;
- token-protected report links;
- email delivery and link recovery;
- print-to-PDF support;
- one time-limited automated rescan;
- analytics, error monitoring, backups, and fulfillment-failure handling;
- clear terms, privacy, refund, and support policies.

### 10.2 Out of Scope

- continuous AI visibility monitoring;
- prompt tracking and share-of-voice analysis;
- account registration and login;
- customer dashboard;
- subscriptions;
- human audits or consulting;
- done-for-you website changes;
- sales calls;
- community submissions;
- sponsor marketplace;
- automated partner matching;
- lead resale;
- agency white labeling;
- bulk projects and workspaces;
- public API;
- multilingual reports;
- guaranteed AI ranking improvements.

Existing sponsor, partner, lead-operations, ROI-calculator, and community features
may remain in the codebase during transition, but they are not part of the core
positioning. They should not control homepage hierarchy, product metrics, or the
main roadmap.

## 11. Core User Journey

1. The customer arrives through a targeted article, comparison page, public
   example report, or direct referral.
2. The customer submits a valid homepage URL.
3. UseGEO scans the page and displays the free evidence-backed result.
4. The customer sees three important problems and previews the paid deliverables.
5. The customer enters an email address and selects two additional pages.
6. UseGEO validates and generates the complete report before payment where
   practical.
7. The customer completes a one-time checkout.
8. A verified payment event unlocks the existing report.
9. UseGEO shows the report immediately and sends a protected link by email.
10. The customer implements the recommendations and can run one automated rescan
    within 30 days.

Generating and persisting the report before payment reduces the risk of accepting
payment and then failing to deliver.

## 12. Experience and Operating Principles

The product must remain viable for one operator.

- No account is required.
- No meeting is required.
- No human review is included.
- One support email handles payment, access, and clear generation failures only.
- Support does not include interpreting marketing strategy or implementing
  recommendations.
- A customer must be able to recover a report link without manual database work.
- Failed scans must block checkout.
- Payment success with delivery failure must trigger a visible recovery or refund
  path.
- Duplicate webhooks must not create duplicate fulfillment.
- Product limitations must be visible before purchase.

## 13. Success Metrics

### 13.1 Product Funnel

- landing page to scan start: greater than 20%;
- scan success rate: greater than 90%;
- scan completion rate: greater than 70%;
- completed scan to checkout start: greater than 5%;
- checkout start to successful payment: greater than 40%;
- completed scan to purchase: initial target greater than 2%.

### 13.2 Commercial Health

- refund rate: below 5%;
- direct report-generation cost: below USD 3;
- gross margin before fixed costs: above 85%;
- paid-but-not-delivered orders: below 1%;
- at least 10 paid purchases from the first 1,000 qualified visits.

### 13.3 Solo-Operator Health

- orders requiring manual intervention: below 5%;
- fewer than one support request per ten orders;
- average weekly manual operations: below three hours;
- no recurring manual fulfillment step in the standard purchase journey.

### 13.4 Validation Decision

After the first 1,000 qualified visits:

- 20 or more purchases: strong signal; continue improving acquisition and report
  quality;
- 10-19 purchases: promising; improve positioning, proof, and paid deliverables;
- 3-9 purchases: weak; revisit target customer and paid value before adding
  features;
- fewer than 3 purchases: stop feature expansion and revalidate the customer,
  problem, and offer.

## 15. Roadmap Implications

The next product work should strengthen the paid deliverable and automated
fulfillment, in this order:

1. align homepage language with the readiness-audit category;
2. separate free diagnosis from paid implementation outputs;
3. support three-page input and validation;
4. generate exact copy, schema, and module recommendations;
5. ensure the full report is generated and persisted before checkout;
6. replace or configure the payment layer for the selected merchant-of-record
   provider;
7. add report-link recovery and one automated rescan;
8. verify analytics, refund handling, monitoring, and backup recovery;
9. publish public example reports for the target customer segment;
10. validate the offer with real paid transactions before building additional
    product categories.

The roadmap should not prioritize dashboards, subscriptions, communities,
partner marketplaces, sponsor tooling, white labeling, or new calculators until
the core paid-report funnel is validated.

## 16. Decision Rule for Future Features

A proposed feature belongs in the core product only if it improves at least one
of the following without materially increasing human support:

1. qualified scan acquisition;
2. evidence credibility;
3. implementation usefulness of the paid report;
4. checkout conversion;
5. successful automated delivery;
6. customer ability to implement and rescan without assistance.

If a feature primarily creates consulting work, partner coordination, moderation,
account administration, or recurring manual fulfillment, it conflicts with this
positioning and should be rejected or kept outside the core product.
