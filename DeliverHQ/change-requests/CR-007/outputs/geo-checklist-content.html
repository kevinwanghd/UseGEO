# Generative Engine Optimization Checklist Use this checklist to see whether your site gives AI systems enough clear, reusable evidence to understand your brand. This is not a magic ranking score. GEO is mostly about making your public pages easier for AI systems to parse, quote, compare, and recommend when someone asks a buying or research question.

## 1. Entity clarity Can a model quickly understand who you are? Check whether your homepage clearly states: - your brand name - your category - who you serve - what problem you solve - what outcome you help create A vague homepage makes it hard for AI systems to place you in a recommendation set. If your page could describe ten different companies, it is probably too vague. ## 2. Content structure Can your page be skimmed and extracted cleanly? Check for: - one clear H1 - useful H2 sections - a title and meta description that reinforce the same idea - short sections with specific claims - internal links to deeper pages AI systems do not need fancy design to understand you. They need structure, names, context, and repeated evidence. ## 3. Proof signals Do you give models something safe to repeat? Look for: - customer examples - case studies - testimonials - numbers or before/after results - named use cases - screenshots or concrete outcomes If your page says “we help teams grow” but never shows who, how, or what changed, the claim is hard to reuse. ## 4. Comparison readiness Can your site answer “why this, not that?” AI recommendation questions often include comparison intent, even when users do not say “versus.” Check for: - alternative pages - comparison pages - pricing or plan context - who you are best for - when you are not the right fit - category tradeoffs If you avoid comparison language, AI systems have less first-party context for recommendation-style answers. ## 5. AI extractability Can crawlers and parsers get the important parts? Check for: - indexable pages - canonical URLs - Organization schema - FAQ schema where useful - visible text, not only images - no accidental noindex tags Technical extractability will not fix weak positioning, but poor extractability can hide otherwise useful content. ## 6. Next-step clarity Does the page tell a visitor what to do next? A good GEO page should not only educate. It should guide the next decision. Examples: - run a scanner - read a comparison guide - review a checklist - request expert follow-up - compare tools or agencies

## What to do after this checklist If you are new to GEO, start with your homepage. It is usually the page AI systems and human buyers use to form the first mental model of your company. After that, look at: - AI visibility tools - GEO agencies and partners UseGEO does not promise that a checklist alone will make AI systems recommend you. The goal is simpler: make your site easier to understand, easier to cite, and easier to evaluate.
